## Information architecture
- Top-level entry point is a per-account "Home" dashboard (image 1), scoped by name in the header title ("Jolene (win) - Main" — a book/data-file and account label, suggesting Quicken's file-then-account hierarchy is surfaced even in the header chrome).
- Home dashboard is composed of independently-drillable report cards (Top Trending Categories, Top Payees, Spending Over Time, Net Income by Month); tapping a card's chart region evidently opens a full-screen report of the same shape (images 2–4 are the same three chart types — spending-over-time bar, category donut, payee donut — shown as standalone screens with a back chevron, confirming card → full report is one tap away).
- Full-screen reports (images 2–4) all share one template: back chevron + title + right-side icon, a horizontal scrollable row of relative/absolute date-range filter pills, a chart, a headline total, and a sortable itemized list below — this is a reused pattern across at least three report types (time, category, payee), implying a single generic "report surface" is one architectural component driving many report entries.
- Budgets (image 5) live in their own top-level surface separate from "reports": month-by-month paging (not a date-range picker), a whole-of-budget progress bar ("Personal Expenses"), then per-category budget lines. Category rows with a trailing chevron (Bills & Utilities, Food & Dining, Home, Kids) are themselves one level deeper (likely subcategory breakdown), while rows without a chevron (Auto & Transport:Gas & Fuel, Cash & ATM, Education, Entertainment, Health & Fitness, Personal Care) are leaf categories with no further drill-down shown.
- Investments (images 6–7) is a third top-level surface with its own internal tab bar (Holdings / Accounts / Watch List), independent of the Budget and Reports areas. A holdings row is one tap from a full single-security quote-and-stats screen (image 7).
- Category naming shows a colon-delimited hierarchy in at least one row: "Auto & Transport:Gas & Fuel" (image 5), indicating parent:child category naming is a first-class, user-visible string format, not just a backend grouping.
- A hamburger menu icon (☰) in the top-left of the Home screen (image 1) is the only visible primary-navigation affordance in this unit; its contents are not shown in any of these seven images.

## Controls inventory
- Hamburger menu icon (☰), top-left, image 1 — presumed primary navigation drawer trigger.
- Back chevron "‹", top-left, images 2–7 — return to previous screen.
- Right-side sliders/filter icon, top-right, images 2–4 — opens additional filter options (contents not shown).
- Date-range filter pill row (images 2–4): "Last 30 Days", "This Month", "Last Month", "Last 3 Months", "Last 6 Months", "Month to Date", "Year to Date", "Custom" — single-select, active pill rendered solid dark-blue with white text, inactive pills light-gray with black text. - Month stepper "‹ Nov 2024 ›" (image 5) — paginate budget month backward/forward.
- Blue "$X out of $Y" inline text on each budget row (image 5) — styling (blue color) implies tappable, likely to edit the allotted budget figure per category.
- Chevron ">" trailing on select budget rows (Bills & Utilities, Food & Dining, Home, Kids in image 5) — drill into subcategory detail; absent on other rows, implying only categories with subcategories get the affordance.
- Tab bar "Holdings / Accounts / Watch List" (image 6) — three-way single-select segmented control, selected tab shown with light/white background.
- Colored day-change "pill" badges throughout (images 4, 6, 7) — not just color-coding text but a distinct rounded-pill background component that always pairs an absolute change with a percent change, separated by "|". Green pill + up-arrow glyph = gain; light-red pill + down-arrow = loss.

- Small circled info icon "ⓘ" next to the sync-status footer text (image 6) — presumed tap target for more detail on sync provenance/timestamp.

## Business rules implied
- A "no category assigned" bucket is a real, ranked, user-facing category called "Uncategorized" that participates in totals and sorts like any other category (image 3, ranked #2 by dollar amount).
- Payee identity can be split by the funding instrument used: the same merchant type recurs as distinct payee rows disambiguated by a trailing "- NNNN" (last-4) suffix, e.g. "United - 9136" vs. presumably a different card's transactions rolling into a different payee bucket (image 4).
- Budgets are evaluated per category per month and can go over 100% of allotment; going over changes both the progress-bar color (green → red) and the status word (Left → Over) rather than just showing a negative "Left" number (image 5, Health & Fitness).
- Category names support an explicit parent:child string format, "Auto & Transport:Gas & Fuel" (image 5), rather than the parent and child being presented as separately nested UI-only labels.
- Portfolio/day-change coloring is tied to daily price direction (red down / green up) applied to the aggregate portfolio value itself, not only to individual holdings — i.e., the top-line net worth-like figure inherits red styling on a down day even though the underlying number is a large positive value (image 6), confirming red is scoped to "movement direction," not to "value sign," in this competitor's system.
- List "sort" state and the descriptive section header are the same piece of state — changing sort presumably rewrites the header text (e.g., "MARKET VALUE DAY CHANGE (HIGH TO LOW)"), rather than sort being a hidden/silent modifier (image 6).
- Reports (spending over time / by category / by payee) all draw from one shared date-range filter vocabulary (the same 8 pills recur verbatim across images 2, 3, 4), implying one shared reporting/date-scoping component underlies all three report types.
- Investment quotes are explicitly timestamped and sourced with a disclosed sync lag: "Last synced from Quicken Desktop 1 day ago" (image 6) and "Updated 1 minute ago" for the option-quote-derived previous close (image 7) — the product distinguishes and separately discloses two different data-freshness clocks (desktop sync vs. quote feed) on data that could otherwise look uniformly "live."

## Standout details
- Pairing an absolute-change figure with a percent-change figure inside one colored pill ("+$1.72 | +1.28%"), used consistently for both individual holdings (image 6) and the top-line portfolio figure (image 6) and repeated on the single-security detail screen (image 7) — a single reusable "delta pill" component doing double duty as color-coding and precise data.
- The Home dashboard's donut-chart card and list-card show the *same five dollar amounts* ($1,750/$186/$175/$135/$75) once as whole dollars (donut legend) and once as cents-precise (payee list) — an implicit rule that headline/glanceable numbers round to whole dollars while itemized/audit-style numbers keep cents, applied consistently across multiple screens in this unit.
- The sort-state text doubling as the list section header ("MARKET VALUE DAY CHANGE (HIGH TO LOW)", "LAST 6 MONTHS") is an efficient way to avoid a separate "currently sorted by" indicator — the label always tells you both scope and order in one string.
- Explicit "Other Payees" (image 4) and "Others" (image 3) aggregate rows keep long-tail categories/payees out of the top-N chart/list while still accounting for 100% of the total — the donut and headline total in image 4 ($22,945) is not just the sum of the named rows; "Other Payees" $11,106.29 absorbs everything below the shown cutoff.
- Disclosed data-provenance footers ("Last synced from Quicken Desktop 1 day ago", "Option quotes report the previous day's close. Updated 1 minute ago.") are a lightweight, textual way of surfacing as-of/staleness information directly in the UI rather than hiding it — relevant precedent for KEEL's reproducible-numbers invariant (BC-v2.1 §9.1), though scoped much more loosely (relative "1 day ago" vs. exact timestamp+source-row citation).
- A visible product/UI typo survived to App Store marketing art: "House Payement" (image 1) — worth noting only as evidence that copy review gaps happen even in shipped assets, not as a pattern to emulate.
