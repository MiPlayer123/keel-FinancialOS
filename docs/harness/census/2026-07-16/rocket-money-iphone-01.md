## Business rules implied
- Budget remaining is computed and displayed pre-summed rather than left for the user to do the subtraction: "$623 left to spend" is shown alongside its inputs "$762 spent" / "$1,385 budgeted" (762+623=1,385, confirms the arithmetic) — evidences a rule that budget cards always surface the derived "left" figure as the primary number (`iphone-02-ASO_iOS_1242x2688_C6.png`).
- A budget period is anchored to a specific named month ("September 2024") with explicit prev/next navigation, implying budgets are strictly monthly and browsable/comparable across months, not a rolling window (`iphone-02-ASO_iOS_1242x2688_C6.png`).
- Net worth is decomposed into exactly two top-level buckets, Assets and Debt, that combine into Net Worth, and each bucket carries its own period-over-period percent-change indicator independent of the others (Assets ↑3%, Debt ↑2%, Net Worth ↑1% are three distinct deltas, not derived from one another in the UI) (`iphone-03-ASO_iOS_1242x2688_C5.png`).
- Derived/aggregate totals (Net Cash on Dashboard, Net Income on Spending, Net Worth row on Net Worth screen) are consistently styled as green text + an (i) info icon with NO chevron, while every linked/navigable account or category row gets a chevron — implying a UI rule distinguishing "computed rollup, tap for methodology" from "linked entity, tap to drill in" (`iphone-05-ASO_iOS_1242x2688_C1.png`, `iphone-06-ASO_iOS_1242x2688_C3.png`, `iphone-03-ASO_iOS_1242x2688_C5.png`).
- Bill-negotiation targets are pre-identified per named merchant with a specific associated date, i.e., the system has already matched recurring/negotiable bills to specific vendors before the user acts, rather than requiring the user to pick a bill to negotiate from a generic list (`iphone-04-ASO_iOS_1242x2688_C4.png`).
- Credit score factors are graded on a discrete letter scale (both visible factors show "A") paired with an "Impact" weight label ("High Impact"), implying the score breakdown model assigns both a grade and an importance weighting per factor (`iphone-07-ASO_iOS_1242x2688_C7.png`).
- The credit score gauge's arc is not fully colored (green for most of the arc, gray for a final short segment near the top-right), implying the gauge encodes a fixed max range with the user's 730 sitting short of the top, i.e., the arc is a fixed scale (e.g., something like 300–850) rather than an auto-scaled range hugging the current value (`iphone-07-ASO_iOS_1242x2688_C7.png`).
- Debt increasing (↑2%) is NOT flagged in a red/negative visual treatment, only in the same warm/tan pill style used elsewhere for non-alarming positive-direction badges — implying the product's color law does not treat "debt went up" as an urgent-red event at this summary altitude (contrast with KEEL's own red-means-negative-money law) (`iphone-03-ASO_iOS_1242x2688_C5.png`).

## Standout details
- Pairing a headline number with a plain-language comparative sentence rather than a bare delta: "$143 below avg. spend" (Dashboard) and "$437 in the last month" (Net Worth) both translate a raw percentage/delta into a reader-friendly sentence next to the number, reducing the need to do mental math.
- The "$48/day for 13d" pill on the Budget spending card converts a lump remaining-budget figure into an actionable daily pacing number with a specified day-count — a small but high-leverage reframing of "how much can I still spend" into "how much per day."
- Two different disclosure affordances used side-by-side on the same screen (Spending: "Earnings ›" vs. "Total Spend ▾") — chevron implies navigating to a new screen, down-caret implies expanding in place — a deliberate (if subtle) signal to the user about what tapping will do before they tap.
- "Include bills" toggle scoped directly above the spending breakdown donut lets the user re-scope a chart's inclusion criteria inline without leaving the screen or opening a settings screen.
- Bottom-tab "More" is used as a home for Net Worth and Credit Score rather than giving either its own top-level tab — keeps the primary tab bar to 5 slots while still surfacing net-worth and credit as first-class features one tap deeper.
- Sync transparency on the Dashboard: an explicit last-synced relative timestamp ("19 hours ago") plus a manual "Sync now" action sits below the account list, giving the user a direct lever and honesty signal about data freshness rather than silently trusting a background sync.
- The exact microcopy "Start a cancelation" uses the single-L American spelling consistently with "cancel"/"canceled" conventions seen elsewhere in the app (worth matching or deliberately diverging from in KEEL copy, since spelling consistency reads as polish).
- Marketing screen 01 offers an explicit "if you'd like to cancel yourself" fallback with alternate contact channels displayed as equal-weight icon buttons alongside the primary "let us do it" CTA — modeling an assisted-action feature that doesn't lock the user into only the automated path.
