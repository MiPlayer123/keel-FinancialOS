## Flow steps
Match-transaction-to-bill sequence (inferred from images 01 → 05 → 02, though not guaranteed to be literally sequential frames of one recorded session):
1. Do: system detects a possible match → See: inline banner under the Twilio row, "This transaction may match to an outstanding bill on NetSuite. Review and match bill" (image 01).
2. Do: user clicks "Review and match" → See: "Match transaction to bill" modal opens over the dimmed table, pre-searched to "Twilio", with the correct bill pre-checked and a live confirmation "Selected bill matches expense amount" (image 05).
3. Do: user clicks "Match & mark ready" → See: modal closes; the row's category cell now shows a dismissible "Matched to Bill #00-598 ×" tag instead of a category dropdown, and its Sync-column button changes from "Mark Ready" to a green "✓ Ready" state (image 02). Undo affordance: the "×" on the match tag (removability implies the match can be undone/edited from the table itself).

AI-approval sequence (inferred pattern from images 04 → 03, on different transaction instances, so treat as an implied general pattern rather than one literal recording):
1. Do: AI evaluates a transaction against policy → See: a "Transaction complete" verdict box with a plain-language rationale and an auto-verified receipt note (image 04); at scale, a system toast reports bulk auto-approvals ("971 transactions were automatically approved by agent … Purchases totaling $84,466.99 were in policy").
2. Do: for a transaction that still needs a human, the item lands on an approval detail screen showing "Awaiting review from {approver}" (image 03).
3. Do: approver reviews Requirements (must show "Complete") and Approvals progress ("0 of 2") → See: "Approve" (⌘+Enter) or "Reject" buttons at the bottom; an approver can instead choose "Request changes" or "Request repay" from a per-approver menu.

## Business rules implied
- A transaction can be matched to one or more outstanding bills, and the match is validated by amount equality before the match is confirmed: "Selected bill matches expense amount" (image 05).
- A matched transaction's category/coding is superseded by the bill match — the category dropdown is replaced by a "Matched to Bill #…" tag, implying bill-matching and category-coding are mutually exclusive/one supersedes the other for that row (image 02).
- Marking a row "Ready" is a distinct, per-row, reversible sync-readiness gate separate from categorization — rows must be explicitly marked ready (or become ready via a bill match) before they participate in "Sync (n)" (images 01/02).
- Expense/transaction approval is a multi-party, ordered, countable workflow — "Approvals 0 of 2" with numbered approver rows, each independently removable or reorderable (image 03).
- A spending source ("Spent from") is set by a named policy/budget object ("Set by General Expenses"), i.e. default funding source is policy-derived, not the submitter's free choice (image 03).
- Receipts can be auto-verified deterministically against three checks — amount, date, merchant — and the system is a "receipt system" that a user can flag as wrong ("Report incorrect receipt verification"), implying auto-verification is contestable, not final (image 06).
- An AI/"agent" can autonomously approve transactions in bulk when they are "in policy," and this is disclosed after the fact with an aggregate dollar total and count, not per-transaction confirmation, unless the user expands it (image 04).
- AI transaction verdicts cite specific evidence for their reasoning (e.g., "related to an active business trip") rather than a bare verdict, and expose a feedback mechanism (thumbs up/down) on that reasoning (image 04).
- Conditional coding rules can restrict which category options are even selectable, based on a dimension like office/location ("Ramp Location is Miami" → restrict displayed NetSuite Category options), with an explicit fallback branch for when no condition matches ("If no Conditions are met → Display All options") (image 07).
- The Policy Agent explicitly declines to guess: "If the policy is unclear or the receipt is ambiguous, the Policy Agent will escalate the expense for manual review rather than guessing" (image 08, AI-authored text — a stated design rule, not directly observed system behavior, so weight accordingly).
- Company policy documents can require employee signatures, and completion is tracked as a literal fraction ("113/295 employees have signed this") (image 08).

## Standout details
- Status/verdict copy is placed immediately under the money headline in both the approval-detail and AI-verdict screens ("$130.00 at Google Domains" → "Awaiting review from…"; "$10.98 at Uber" → "Transaction complete") — a strict status-adjacent-to-the-number convention.
- Keyboard-shortcut affordances are surfaced directly inside primary buttons as visible key badges: "Next [J]", "Approve [⌘][↵]" — teaches power users the shortcut in place rather than in a separate help panel.
- AI-suggested categorization is marked with a distinct lightning-bolt icon on the category value itself (image 01/02), visually distinguishing "AI-set" fields from plain data inline, without needing a separate column or badge.
- The bulk-auto-approval disclosure is deliberately a dismissible, collapsible aggregate ("971 transactions… $84,466.99… Collapse") rather than surfacing each of the 971 approvals individually — progressive disclosure of an otherwise overwhelming audit trail.
- The receipt "Auto-verified" claim is backed by a lightweight, specific popover (three named checks) plus a direct "report incorrect" contest link right there — trust-but-verify micro-copy pattern.
- The match-to-bill flow validates and states the exact reason a match is safe ("Selected bill matches expense amount") rather than silently allowing the match — reduces user doubt at the exact decision point.
- Rule builder communicates branching logic with distinct icon semantics: a fork/branch icon for "If", a funnel icon for the resulting "Display" filter, a triangle/warning icon for the "else" (no-match) branch — icon-carries-meaning without needing text labels to distinguish branch types.
- Overflow is handled with a "+1 more" chip on multi-select fields rather than wrapping or truncating awkwardly (image 07).
- The merchant-logo checkerboard placeholder pattern is used consistently in place of missing brand art, functioning as a neutral "no logo available" texture rather than a blank or generic-letter avatar.
- The subtitle in the match-to-bill modal literally reads "{{ERP name}}" — an unrendered template token caught on camera — evidence the underlying copy is templated per-ERP-integration (e.g., would read "NetSuite" for a NetSuite-connected workspace) but the template failed to interpolate in this particular capture.
