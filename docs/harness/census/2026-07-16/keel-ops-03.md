## Business rules implied
- Reimbursements are structurally distinct from income: "the incoming money settles the claim instead of counting as income" (reimbursements-desktop.png) — implies a dedicated claim/settlement object rather than posting incoming reimbursement cash as an income transaction.
- Statements drive period-locking: "Enter a bank statement, reconcile it against the ledger, lock the period" (statements-desktop.png) — implies reconciliation against an entered statement is the mechanism that locks a ledger period.
- Categorization rules are overridden by manual edits: "KEEL sets the category and/or a friendly name — automatically, on every sync. Your own edits always win." (settings-desktop.png) — explicit precedence: rule-applied values are re-applied every sync, but user edits take priority over rule output.
- The audit trail is user-visible and described as append-only: "Every change — yours or KEEL's — is recorded in an append-only audit trail." (settings-desktop.png) — matches the append-only audit_log requirement, and is surfaced directly to the end user, not just internally.
- Bank connections are managed and act independently per institution: separate "active" status, "Sync now," and "Disconnect" controls per row with no bulk/global sync-all control shown (connections-desktop.png).
- Export is explicitly framed as a standing guarantee/feature: "Your full financial record, any time — the exit door is a feature." (settings-desktop.png) — though the visible format set on this screen is JSON/QIF/Beancount only, with no CSV button captured.

- Empty states across the app share one consistent illustrated pattern (dashed border, centered icon matching the page's own concept, bold headline, muted instructional subtext) — evidenced identically on Reimbursements and Statements, suggesting a reusable empty-state component.
