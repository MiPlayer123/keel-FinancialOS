## Business rules implied
- Spend total excludes certain categories from its headline number: the subtitle "Monthly spend not including recurrings left to pay" (x06) states unpaid/upcoming recurring charges are not counted in the "$3,562.07" figure — implying recurring bills are tracked/forecast separately from realized spend.
- Spend categories are partitioned into at least three tiers with different treatment: "Negative spend categories" (e.g., Entertainment $48.69 — likely refunds/credits within a normally-debit category, shown in green suggesting a credit), plain "Categories" (normal debit spend), and "Excluded" (e.g., Work Expenses $53.20 — visually de-emphasized/low-contrast, implying these dollars are tracked but not counted toward personal-spend totals, likely because they're reimbursable) (x06).
- Historical "Key Metrics" are computed per calendar year and shown even for years with $0 (2022), implying the metric renders for the full range of an account's connection history rather than only years with data (x06).
- Investment accounts distinguish a "Balance" view from a "Performance" view as alternate chart modes for the same account, selectable via a persistent per-account setting (x08) — implying balance-over-time and time-weighted-return-over-time are computed/stored as distinct series.
- A holding (MSFT) can be held across multiple linked accounts, and the holding-detail screen aggregates/lists "Positions" per contributing account with that account's own share count and value (x10, x11) — implying position-level data preserves which brokerage account contributed which shares (relevant to KEEL's per-account/source provenance).
- Price data carries an explicit as-of/staleness marker — "Price at 01:00 PM 🌙" (x10) and "Price at 10:33 AM" (x11), plus per-account "Updated" timestamp (x08) and "1 hour ago" per-position freshness (x11) — implying the product surfaces data recency at multiple granularities (whole-portfolio, per-account, per-position) rather than a single global "last synced" indicator.
- Total return is shown as both an absolute dollar figure and a percentage in the same line ("$505.20 (25.31%)", x10), implying the underlying data model tracks both cost basis and current value per holding to compute return in both units simultaneously.

## Standout details
- The 4-way color binding in the spend category list (colored dot + emoji + colored label text + tinted row background, all sharing one hue per category) is a strong "scan fast" affordance — a user can identify a category by color alone from across the room, and the emoji adds a second, language-independent identifier (x06).
- "Excluded" and "Negative spend" categories get materially different visual treatment (muted/low-opacity for Excluded vs. full-saturation green for Negative) even though both are "not counted normally" — this distinguishes "doesn't count because it's not yours to count" (Excluded/reimbursable) from "counts, but as a credit" (Negative) (x06).
- The dashed/dotted vs. solid bar convention in the spend sparkline (x06) quietly encodes "before this account had data" vs. "real recorded spend" without any text label — a purely geometric/opacity signal.
- The moon emoji next to "Price at 01:00 PM 🌙" (x10) is a compact, wordless way of flagging after-hours/stale pricing rather than writing "market closed" — icon-as-microcopy.
- The Allocation panel (x09) uses a single "primary" dark color (Equity, the largest bar) against a shared secondary gray-blue for all other categories, rather than a unique hue per slice — a deliberate "highlight the biggest one, group the rest" palette strategy that avoids a rainbow effect for 3+ categories.
- Segmented timeframe selectors are visually identical (pill-shaped, gray-highlighted active state) across very different chart types (net-worth/balance in x08, price-in-a-modal in x10, price-in-a-card in x11) — a single reusable control pattern applied everywhere a time series appears.
- x11's positions row packs four data points into one line (account name, relative freshness, share quantity, dollar value) while x10's compresses the equivalent to a single share-count number — the same underlying data is presented at different densities depending on screen real estate/breakpoint, suggesting a deliberate "more room = more columns" responsive rule rather than a fixed table schema.
- The account-options dropdown menu in x08 groups "Balance/Performance" (a content-mode toggle, radio-style with a checkmark) separately from "Display options" and "Account management" (which are navigational, marked with trailing chevrons) — a menu that mixes an inline toggle with drill-down links in one surface, visually distinguished by the checkmark-vs-chevron affordance.
