## Business rules implied
- flow-17: Individual notification types can each independently enable a different subset of channels (Email / Push (Mobile) / In app) — e.g. "Planned expense nearing or over limit," "Refund status," and "Spending update" omit Email and only use Push (Mobile) + In app, while most others (High credit card balance, Income received, Large deposit, Large transaction, Low bank account balance, Monthly summary, Projected low bank account balance, Uncategorized transactions) include all three channels. This shows channel-eligibility is defined per notification-type, not uniformly.
- flow-18: A user can multi-select transactions across the register and see a running signed-dollar total for the selection alongside the count (`(-$142.15) 5 transactions selected`), implying the app computes an aggregate on the fly per selection, and the sign of that aggregate is preserved (negative here, rendered in parenthesized-negative-style formatting rather than a red color in this crop).
- flow-19: Category Group has exactly three mutually exclusive states with directly stated arithmetic/sign consequences: "Expenses" treats both incoming and outgoing transactions as spending; "Earnings" treats transactions as income and counts only positive transactions; "Ignored" excludes all transactions from calculations entirely. This is a directly quoted business rule about how category-group assignment changes what counts in reports.
- flow-19: Tax-deductibility is a category-level flag ("Mark as Tax Deductible?"), not a transaction-level flag, implied by its placement on the category-creation form.
- flow-20: Different institution "types" expose different primary reconnect/refresh actions — a full bank connection offers "Add Account" (implying more accounts can be pulled from the same linked institution), whereas the Apple Card connection offers "Import Statement" instead (implying that institution's data arrives via statement import rather than incremental account discovery/sync).
- flow-20: "Unlink" is available per institution (not per account), implying unlinking is an institution-level operation that would presumably remove all accounts under that institution together.

## Standout details
- flow-17: The subtitle line under each notification name doubles as a compact "which channels are active" summary without needing to open the row — e.g. "Email, Push (Mobile), In app" versus "Push (Mobile), In app" — so users can visually scan channel differences across notification types without drilling into each one.
- flow-18: The bulk-selection affordance folds the running dollar total directly into the "N selected" label rather than as a separate summary line, so the financial consequence of the current selection is inseparable from the count itself: `(-$142.15) 5 transactions selected`.
- flow-18: The upcoming-bills carousel uses a single-letter colored-square "avatar" (I, S, S) as a lightweight merchant identity marker in a dense horizontal strip, paired with relative/human due-date phrasing ("In 3 days") rather than only absolute dates, mixed with absolute dates ("on Apr 6") for items presumably further out — a hybrid relative/absolute date-labeling convention.
- flow-19: The category-group picker doesn't just label the three choices, it explains the downstream arithmetic effect of each choice in a one-line caption, directly inside the picker sheet — e.g. "Both incoming and outgoing transactions will be treated as spending" — which functions as inline just-in-time education about ledger consequences rather than requiring separate help text.
- flow-19: "Ignored" is visually distinguished with red/warning-colored text for its title (versus black for "Expenses" and "Earnings"), signaling its exclusion effect through color alone, in addition to its copy.
- flow-20: The "Add Institution" CTA is the only solid/filled black control on an otherwise all-outlined-or-text-link screen, making it unmistakably the primary/terminal action distinct from all the per-institution maintenance actions above it.
- flow-21: The interstitial explicitly names the destination app in its own copy ("Continue to Rocket Money to finish account linking") rather than generic phrasing, reducing user confusion about why they're bouncing through a third-party domain (`cdn.plaid.com`) mid-flow.
