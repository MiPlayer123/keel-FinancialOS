## Business rules implied
- Recurring items carry a frequency (Monthly/Weekly/Annual) and a linked account (masked last-4 + institution icon), and are due-dated relative to "today" rather than shown as absolute calendar dates (images 01, 03, 08).
- The mobile app buckets recurring items into two distinct types with independent counts and independent annualized totals: "5 SUBSCRIPTIONS … $4,113 per year" and "5 BILLS & UTILITIES … $4,095 per year" (image 08) — subscriptions and bills/utilities are modeled as separate categories, not one undifferentiated "recurring" bucket.
- By contrast, the web "All Recurring" tab shows a single combined count/total ("10 SUBSCRIPTIONS … You spend $5,474/yearly") for a list that includes items resembling bills (e.g. "Capital One Membership Fee," "Check") under the same "SUBSCRIPTIONS" label (image 01) — the web and mobile surfaces do not evidence the same categorical split for the same underlying data model (or the sample accounts simply differ; see Open questions).
- Row actions distinguish "Cancel [Name]" (act on the subscription with the provider) from "Remove From List" (delist from tracking only) as two separate, non-equivalent operations (image 04).
- Subscription cancellation performed by Rocket Money requires the user to supply identity and billing details (name, email, phone, billing address, last-4 of card) and to explicitly consent to Rocket Money representing them as the account holder to the provider — a documented authorization step precedes any provider contact (image 05).
- Bill negotiation is charged as a contingent/success fee expressed as a percentage of savings ("Negotiation fee: 50% savings"), billed via a differently-named line item ("Rocket Money BN") than the original biller, and disclosed to the user before that charge appears (image 06).
- Bill negotiation offers two acceptable evidence/auth paths to proceed: live provider credentials (Username/Password) or a submitted bill document (screenshot/PDF) (image 07) — the system does not require credential-sharing as the only path.
- The category-spend donut always highlights one dominant category by amount at its center with a period-over-period percentage delta (here "CREDIT CARD PAYMENT $636.28, ↓ 17% from Jun") (image 02) — the "biggest slice" gets a distinguished center-of-chart treatment rather than all categories being equal.
- Spending-insight narration attributes a month-over-month total change to the sum of category-level deltas by name and dollar amount ("$697 more this month due to increases in Shopping (up by $293), Uncategorized (up by $293) and Groceries (up by $148)") (image 02) — implies a reproducible per-category delta computation feeding the sentence, not just a total.

## Standout details
- Merchant name is interpolated directly into the cancel action's label ("Cancel Netflix" rather than a generic "Cancel Subscription") — concrete, reduces ambiguity about what will be canceled (image 04).
- The bill-negotiation upsell is surfaced twice on the same page at once — a modal and a persistent right-rail card — both pointing at the same action (image 03).
- Social proof is personalized and dollar-specific rather than generic: "Cameron just saved $120.00 by lowering his DirectTV bill." (image 03) — a first name + exact figure reads more credible than aggregate marketing copy alone.
- The submission confirmation proactively discloses the future statement descriptor by exact string ("Rocket Money BN") so the user recognizes the eventual charge and doesn't dispute it — disclosure happens at confirmation time, before the charge posts (image 06).
- A low-friction escape hatch ("Upload your bill instead") is offered directly beneath the credential form for users unwilling to share provider logins, rather than only after a failed login attempt (image 07).
- Each donut-chart category segment carries its own small icon-in-circle glyph (cart, bag, tools, banknote, question mark, swap-arrows) embedded in the ring itself, serving as an inline legend so users don't need to cross-reference a separate key (image 02).
- The "Change" table column header carries an inline "(i)" info-icon affordance, keeping a definitional tooltip available without cluttering the header row with explanatory text (image 02).
- Calendar widget uses a minimal dot-under-date convention to flag "has scheduled item" rather than showing counts or previews inline (image 03).
- Confetti + "We're on it!" framing treats "request submitted" as the celebratory moment, ahead of any confirmed savings outcome — an example of front-loading positive affect at the point of user commitment rather than at task completion (image 06).
